It is obviously use case specific but for multiple projects that I have been presented with it has made a logical choice. Salesforce communities are very powerful but IMHO don’t have the best reputation with business stakeholders who feel inclined to follow the pack of people building their “customer 360” experience on something like AWS/Azure/Heroku etc. 

I have been part of many projects NOT following this architecture. The amount of resources and time necessary to effectively replicate Salesforce data into another data store seems completely unnecessary usually. Especially if you can afford to use experience cloud licensing for user management. 

I also have seen a major repeated trend that Salesforce developers are very expensive and frankly speaking, not the best front end devs I have ever worked with. 

Pairing a good react developer with a good Salesforce architect has been an intriguing combination… at least for me!

As always use your architectural best practices to make the right decision. Just remember that business stakeholders often care about cost and speed to market above everything.
