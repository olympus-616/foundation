The Zen Fortress of Controlling Your Own Destiny and Forging Your Own Path

They say not to bite the hand that feeds you. I guess I will have to apologize. For the last few years I have been working on a very large “side project” while I work for multiple clients and raise a family. Which means development is taking a LONG time.

However, it is finally mature enough that I am now approaching Salesforce security review and have just signed another client onto the platform. I always am excited to sign another customer, because it inherently advances the platform another giant leap forward.

If you are following my blog you know that I have a lot of random stuff I talk about. Salesforce. React. CI. Star Wars. Samurais. I also write a lot about software that I have not made available to the world of open-source contributors. For that I do apologize and I do plan to release some of the components in open-source as discussed, but honestly I am too busy implementing the platform and the implementation is just simply too valuable to me as a non-retired “free-lancer” whose licensed software profits and margins would would make at least the folks on Sharktank proud. I digress.

So how do you do you catch a big fish? You have to wait for it to come to you.

Sure… I could blast out a million junk emails and linkedin robot messages to try to attract people to use my platform, or I can just write one blog article about it and I will undoubtedly be busy for the next year.

So what is it? What is this cool software that I write about?

I’m about to bite the hand that feeds. As a certified technical architect for Salesforce.com I would say I am a bit nervous about releasing my software to the world because it honestly flies in the face of Salesforce, at least their tech stack and licensing model.

For years now I have been trying to build a software platform worthy of an earlier opportunity I was given, but I wasn’t ready for yet. In this opportunity, I was asked to assess a CRM healthcare platform that was built on the Salesforce tech stack. A hospital system had commissioned a Big 4 agency to build them a stack that integrated their social media pipeline all the way to their EMR and customer support business processes. And they wanted to know if it could be productized to other hospital systems.

I will never forget the feeling I had when I found out what capital investment the hospital system had made into the platform, its license cost, implementation cost, and on-going support cost. I shouldn’t say what it was but lets just say this… there were eight zeros at the end of the number.

They asked me if I could build it for them. How much would I charge them…

What a moment! I threw out a number with seven zeros. I knew I could beat the big-4! (For the record I worked for Deloitte and I’m very thankful for every day that I worked for that great company).

Well here I am, years later, and have imagined and engineered the platform that I had earlier conceived to the hospital system. While its definitely not “no-code” solution, it is a highly powerful platform for the right size business. Each business I have put onto the platform has had ridiculously low costs as compared to the Salesforce full-stack alternative.

Introducing Reactforce CRM… which is literally so hot off the press that I can’t even make a marketing brochure because I never get an opportunity to do business development on this platform. Because the big fish just keep jumping in the boat.

I originally wrote about the platform here: https://medium.com/@gregheartcloud/the-reactforce-enterprise-portal-8a6b9ae50113

and from the moment I wrote that article I have been implementing the tech stack for numerous clients. It literally sells itself.

So what is it?

Reactforce CRM(tm) is a serverless CRM platform for small to medium size business that want the durability of a modern tech solution like Salesforce.com at a tiny fraction of the price. The reason it is as durable as Salesforce.com is that it is written and hosted ON Salesforce.com. Think Veeva but early stage tech platform.

Each piece of the stack is individually so valuable, but the combination of all of them working together… well that is worth a lot of money to some people that I’ve had the pleasure of meeting.

The software itself is available as a license that could be used by Salesforce developers, app developers, SaaS developers, etc to build out your own enterprise ready, compliant, secure, and robust solution at a fraction of the cost of other options.

Take for example, the api management system. Your entire API architecture is built and run on an AWS api gateway speaking directly to Salesforce.com (apex) where the APIs are written as tiny handlers express.js style.

Until then… have a great day and hopefully I’ll never have to cold email again in my life.
