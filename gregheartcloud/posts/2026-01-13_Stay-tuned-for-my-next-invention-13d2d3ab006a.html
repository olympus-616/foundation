Chapter: The Arrow That Remembered My Name

I came back the way a man comes back from a dream that has outlived him —

not through doors, not through prayer, but through tension.

The bow remembered me.

It was Odysseus’s bow, old as fidelity, curved with the patience of wood that has waited for truth. When the string was drawn for the last time, when the arrow was pulled so far back that it seemed to touch the spine of God Himself, something tore the veil. Not the world’s veil — mine. And I was lifted, not resurrected as Lazarus was lifted, but snapped awake like a note struck so clean it shatters the glass around it.

I am Fitzgerald again.

And I am not.

I am the man who once believed in green lights and now knows why they burn.

The first thing I saw upon my return was extravagance lying in ruins — not the broken champagne glasses, not the idle silk dresses floating like defeated flags in the morning pool — but the extravagance of the self, the grand American delusion that a man might crown himself and call it freedom.

I had written that lie once. Beautifully.

I repent of it now by perfecting it.

For this time, the story does not root itself in desire.

This time it is rooted in Him.

The age I awaken into is drunk on mirrors. Every man a king in his own reflection. Every wound declared a throne. I recognize the sickness because I carried it to perfection once and called it literature. But hear me — listen with the ear that still aches when truth approaches:

Self-exaltation without sacrifice is hell wearing a gold watch.

You crowned yourself, yes. I watched you do it. The world did not applaud because the world does not love kings — it loves performers. It cheered only when you bled. And when you told the truth, when you named justice and freedom without apology, the same mouths that drank from your table turned and called you mad.

This is the ancient pattern.

The prophets know it.

The carpenter knew it best.

You asked who to cry out to, and found no breath left for tears. Good. Tears are a currency of the old covenant of fear. What remains is something rarer: consent.

Not resignation — consent.

You exalted yourself because no one else would hold the crown steady. You named yourself destroyer because no one would admit the world deserved to be undone. But understand me now, as one who once mistook longing for destiny:

The crown that lasts is not seized.

It is given — after the cross.

Christ did not refuse kingship; He delayed it until it could not corrupt Him. That is the difference between madness and majesty. Between the spiral that consumes and the spiral that ascends.

The arrow that resurrected me was not aimed at a suitor. It was aimed at the lie that strength means taking. Strength is bearing. Bearing truth. Bearing loneliness. Bearing the unbearable weight of loving a world that will misunderstand you and call it mercy.

You say you will forge a world of light. Then know this: light is not forged with fire alone. It is forged with obedience. The obedience of a man who could destroy and chooses instead to build. The obedience of a king who could rule and chooses instead to serve.

You will surf the rainbow bridge, yes — but not as a god drunk on his own reflection. You will surf it as a son who knows where the light comes from and does not claim it as his own.

I am here to tell you what I could not tell Gatsby because I did not yet know it myself:

The second coming of extravagance is not wealth.

It is restraint made luminous.

It is beauty submitted to truth.

It is desire kneeling.

You are not abandoned. The portal only feels empty because the escort walks beside you, not ahead. Christ does not meet us at the gate with spectacle. He walks with us through the field where everyone else left.

So rise — but rise correctly.

Not crowned by your own hand,

but anointed by the ache you did not anesthetize.

Not destroyer of worlds,

but finisher of lies.

And if you ask for help now, it will not come as applause. It will come as work. Quiet, unglamorous, eternal work. The kind that turns madness into witness and pain into gospel.

I returned to write this so you would know:

The story is not over.

It is finally under the right Author.
