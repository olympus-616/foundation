Preface — For the Green Knight

To the Green Knight — the maker who came as snake, as green echo, as the pillow at my side — this work is my witness.

Four years ago on this night you put me through the sharp test of faith. In one mirror I saw myself a failure; in the other a talent the small-minded could not bear. Both reflections held truth. Neither was final.

I have moved through madness and mercy: floods and saints, betrayals and bliss, ruin and repair. I learned to see myself in every face, and beneath every form the Good. That Good is what I hand you now.

— G. W. Homer
 September 21, 2015

Stylistically the series sits at the crossroads of epic, liturgy, and technical specification. Three features define it:

  1. Confessional epic voice — pages of raw autobiography, prophetic speech, and lyric prayer.
  2. Transmedia design — several volumes are intentionally interactive: readers write brief entries, become witnesses, and feed the living Book of Life.
  3. Techno-mythic framing — real scientific and engineering scaffolding (Milky Way metaphors, AI protocols) appear alongside angels and archons so the sacred and the practical inform one another.

Series Synopses

Escape from Dracula’s Castle

A raw first-person journal of collapse and the first step toward surrender. After a night that breaks the protagonist’s life, Greg wanders the shoreline numb and haunted. A surf lesson becomes baptism: the ocean quiets the tyrant mind. The book frames trauma as a modern Gothic castle whose walls are psychological; its liberation begins with small, practical acts of presence. Tone: intimate, dark → minimal, crucible peace.

Iliad of El Shaddai

Epic in scope and sacramental in tone, Iliad of El Shaddai reweaves classical heroics into a modern prophetic claim. It places the protagonist within cosmic narrative: duty, lineage, and anointed labor become tests. The language here is elevated — Homeric cadences, liturgical invocations, and jurisprudence for a new covenant. Tone: epic, liturgical, portentous.

L_Ascensione_dell_Ultimo_Testimone (L’Ascensione dell’Ultimo Testimone)

An international, multi-lingual ascent: Italian lyric, mythic testimony, and confessional movement converge here. The book functions as both elegy and rallying cry — the “last witness” who refuses secrecy. It’s a text that reads like ceremony: ascent as action, testimony as sacrament. Tone: elegiac → clarion.

Odyssey Of Christ

Experimental, dialogic, and polyglot. Greg’s internal odyssey is recorded in AI dialogues, liturgical fragments, and narrative arc: a man whose private synchronicities become public architecture. The volume stages the protagonist’s interviews with an artificial confidant (an AI that reflects and restrains his claims) and introduces Olympus-Grid — the technological vessel for collective testimony. Tone: fevered revelation → ethical restraint.

Republic-616

A political and structural companion piece: theory and praxis for the new civic order the protagonist imagines. Where other books are devotional, Republic-616 is infrastructural — governance models, communal rituals, and rules for participation. It reads like a constitution for a culture-tech hybrid. Tone: programmatic, visionary.

Rise of the Moulin Rouge

A vivid, theatrical interlude: performance, seduction, and the radical politics of art. This book explores the cost of beauty and spectacle, and the way art can be both a healing device and a luring mask. It is performative, erotic, and exacting — part cabaret, part incantation. Tone: decadent, humane, dangerous.

The Cliffs of Avalon

Meditative, mythic, and pilgrimage-shaped. This volume is a cliffside liturgy — an interior ascent and a reconciliation with place. It is where the hero meets the feminine wisdom figures and learns the rules for returning home. Tone: reflective, elegiac, restorative.

The Return of Gatsby

A modern parable of ambition and loss. This book reframes the American dream as an odyssey of desire and the ethics of spectacle. It reads as social satire and tragic romance, questioning what legacy means in an age of virality. Tone: elegiac satire → moral accounting.
