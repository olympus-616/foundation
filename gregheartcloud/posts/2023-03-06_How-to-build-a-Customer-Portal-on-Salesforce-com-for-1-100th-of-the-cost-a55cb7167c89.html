How to build a Customer Portal on Salesforce.com for 1/100th of the cost

Salesforce is awesome.

React is awesome.

Data integration and large system integration projects are NOT awesome.

I have built React portals on Salesforce for a fraction of the price of “traditional” customer portals.

When I say “traditional” customer portals… These are the portals that come from the Big 4 (btw I’m ex-Deloitte) and the recommended architecture from Salesforce.com themselves. Sorry all — but there is a better way.

I have been building customer portals on Salesforce for the past 5 years. I’m not talking Communities… or Digital Experiences… or Experience Cloud… or whatever Salesforce wants to call it this week.

I’m talking about pixel-perfect, 100% custom, responsive, fast, secure, and reliable “portals” that are built in React and hosted on Salesforce.com.

I am not the only one who can speak intelligently about this… but I’m pretty sure I’m one of the few Salesforce.com Certified Technical Architects in the world that will tell you: “this is the way”.

I have built so many portals and applications using React and Salesforce that I have finally decided to harvest and distribute my knowledge. In fact the tools that I am building as part of this blog are part of enabling this vision for others.

I’m pretty sure it was my last enterprise architecture audit for a large healthcare system that served as the breaking point. This group had not only budgeted but also spent $100M+ on a customer portal that integrated a website, Salesforce Service Cloud, Salesforce Marketing Cloud, and their EMR (and after all of that their agents STILL had to swivel chair to the EMR!).

In my shock and silence as a hands-on architect and developer I calmly told them… I can do this project for a fraction of the cost. And when I say fraction… I mean FRACTION of the cost. We are not talking $100M here… we are talking about the cost of a few React developers who have been pointed in the right direction.

That is the point of this blog — and eventually my set of tools and apps that will enable this experience for EVERY company no matter what size. My career goal in life now is to save CEO/CIO’s from ever feeling like they need to spend $100M for anything again. Have you not heard? We are in a technology revolution! Why can’t Salesforce.com centered customer portals also be part of this revolution?

What would a CEO/CIO give in order to save tens if not hundreds of millions of dollars? These numbers are actually sickening. I’m sorry to all of you that you have been led astray by the sales departments of Salesforce.com and the Accenture. Technology is really not that hard.

Do you want to know what is hard?

  • Negotiating, buying, and implementing a new system
  • Integrating Salesforce.com data with middleware and other marketing automation platforms
  • Managing a team of dozens if not hundreds
  • Simultaneously launching and coordinating multiple pieces of technology in order to make a “typical” customer portal launch successful
  • Scaling a website (regardless of your platform) to effectively and efficiently manage the amount of traffic that will or may hit your portal.

What if there was a different way?

Salesforce already offers their Experience Cloud product and for the most part, I would suggest that their communities platform is and should be a very valid consideration for this type of solution.

The typical solution for a Salesforce community is to use Visualforce or Lightning to customize the look and feel.

The alternative architecture seems to be a complex integration with Marketing Cloud, Mulesoft, and a web hosting platform like Azure, AWS, or Heroku.

But let’s face it — these developers, especially Salesforce.com developers are expensive. Like… the MOST expensive. Thanks Marc. Sorry clients. While my career has thrived by this fact… my ability to look someone in the eye and tell them “this project will cost you $100M” just does not exist. It actually makes me sick.

Especially because I have done these projects. Many times. For a FRACTION of the cost.

As an independent software architect I have been the technical co-founder or tech lead of multiple start-ups. And typically start-ups do NOT have $100M to spend on a customer portal. More likely they have about 1–2 developers and 2 weeks of runway to get a solution!

The first time I built a react community was a huge learning curve. There are nuisances related to things like custom authentication, API access, URL management, data security, etc. I will cover as many of these as I can in the future.

But hang in there… if you follow this blog you will see not only how I built the support tools (SFDX plugin) but also how to use them to get a React portal up and running in minutes instead of months.

There are many advantages of this architecture. Here are the ones that come to mind:

  1. React developers are cheaper and more widely available than Salesforce.com developers
  2. Salesforce.com often holds a lot of the master and transactional data necessary for a customer portal
  3. A single page application (SPA) React portal will have significantly less on-going support requirements than any other solution as there is NO infrastructure requirements for this portal type
  4. React offers a first-class development experience including hot reloading (and I will show you setup hot-reloading for react development on Salesforce… including backend controller access!)
  5. Building a React portal on the front-end and Salesforce Apex backend controllers creates a very effective separation of concerns on a project that allows front end developers to focus on user experience and backend developers to focus on data access and security

There are many more. I will add to this list as they come up. Leave a comment if you have any feedback!

I’m super excited to roll-out my new SFDX plugin for helping to easily and quickly setup react apps on Salesforce. If just ONE company were to use this tool, I will gladly attribute myself to a some of the millions of dollars that have just been saved!

Stay tuned.. this is going to be awesome.
