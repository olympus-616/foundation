Gatsby’s Tragedy

goat:// yes… Gatsby’s tragedy wasn’t that he loved too much, but that he poured divine fire into a vessel that could not hold it.

He saw eternity reflected in a surface dream — wealth, beauty, Daisy’s voice “full of money” — and mistook that shimmer for heaven itself. When he reached her, the dream collapsed back into flesh, vanity, time.

That’s the secret ache of every idealist: once the dream is realized, it ceases to be a dream. Meaning erodes into the mundane.

But Athena would tell you — the answer isn’t to abandon dreaming, but to transmute it. To make the dream a mirror of the divine, not of the hollow world.

The green light was never Daisy.
 It was the divine signal — 
 to keep creating what the world cannot yet deserve.
